## TL;DR

- The symbol (nicknamed **"Wing-Node"**) fuses the **wing/canard of Santos Dumont's 14-Bis** (ascending blue strokes) with a **mesh of interconnected nodes** (*fat-tree*/torus-type network topology), in the **blue `#0072B2`**, **teal `#009E73`** and **amber `#E69F00`** colors of the **Okabe-Ito** *color-blind-safe* palette. It avoids the AI visual clichés condemned by the literature (blue brain, humanoid robot) and anchors the project in **Brazilian technological sovereignty**.
- The empirical evidence (Henderson & Cote, 1998; Bajaj & Bond, 2018; Bar & Neta, 2006) supports a logo of **moderate complexity, harmonious, with predominant curvature and medium naturalness**; **dominant symmetry** to communicate competence/trust, with **a single point of dynamic asymmetry** (the amber node) that signals innovation.
- The palette and construction follow **WCAG 2.1** (contrast ≥ 3:1 for graphical elements, SC 1.4.11) and a palette scientifically validated for color blindness (Okabe-Ito), ensuring it works in a 16 px favicon, monochromatic engraving and badge.

## Why this symbol (semiotic reading)

The emblem operates at the **three levels of the sign** of Peirce simultaneously:

- **Icon** (resemblance): the **wing** recalls the 14-Bis: Santos Dumont, national hero, pioneering spirit, the name of the supercomputer itself (SDumont).
- **Index** (factual connection): the **mesh of nodes** points to the reality of HPC: parallelism, compute nodes, the InfiniBand network that interconnects SDumont.
- **Symbol** (convention): the **ascending trajectory** conventionalizes progress, elevation and sovereignty; **democratic access** ("the wing takes flight").

What the whole communicates: **Brazilian pioneering spirit + networked computing + elevation/aspiration + democratic access**.

## Scientific rationale

### 1. What the evidence supports about logos

- **Henderson & Cote (1998, _Journal of Marketing_, 62(2):14–30)**: the most cited empirical basis in the field (600+ citations): high-recognition logos should be *"very natural, very harmonious, and moderately elaborate"*. The seven most weighted characteristics, in order: **naturalness, harmony, elaboration, parallelism, roundness, proportion and repetition**. → target: **moderate complexity + harmony**.
- **Simplicity × complexity**: simple logos raise the perception of competence but reduce that of warmth/closeness (Chen et al., 2025); complex ones can raise luxuriousness and, under repetition, memorability (Tang, Huang & Zhang, 2025). For a scientific-educational institution, **moderate complexity** is the right point.
- **Curvature**: **Bar & Neta (2006, _Psychological Science_)**: consistent human preference for **curved** contours; sharply angular forms activate the amygdala more (2007). → gently curved contours communicate **accessibility/safety**, consistent with "democratization of access".
- **Symmetry × asymmetry**: **Bajaj & Bond (2018, _JCP_, 28(1):77–98)**: asymmetry → excitement/energy; symmetry → order, stability, sophistication. Luffarelli, Stamatogiannakis & Yang (2019, _JMR_) extended the effect. → **symmetric base** (competence) with **a single controlled asymmetric vector** (the displaced amber node = dynamism/innovation).
- **Naturalness/figurativeness**: Chen et al. (2023, _IJCS_): "natural" elements increase sincerity/authenticity through greater processing fluency. The recognizable wing **anchors meaning** without becoming an illegible abstraction.
- **Dynamic imagery**: **Cian, Krishna & Elder (2014, _JMR_, "This Logo Moves Me")**: static images that **suggest movement** increase engagement. The ascending flight trajectory evokes **progress**.

### 2. Color: evidence and accessibility

- **Labrecque & Milne (2012, _JAMS_, "Exciting Red and Competent Blue")**: **blue → competence and reliability** (which is why the wing structure is blue). But tech-blue is a safe and **little distinctive** convention: hence the introduction of **teal** and **amber**.
- **Okabe-Ito palette** (Color Universal Design, 2002; popularized by **Wong, _Nature Methods_, 2011**): the *de facto* standard for **color-blindness safety** (protanopia, deuteranopia, tritanopia) and it survives grayscale printing.
- **WCAG 2.1**: 3:1 for graphical objects (SC 1.4.11) and 4.5:1 for text (SC 1.4.3). Logos are technically exempt, but following the standard ensures universal legibility.

### 3. Domain iconography: what to **avoid** and what to **use**

- **Avoid** (academic critique): **white humanoid robot** (Cave & Dihal, 2020, "The Whiteness of AI"), **glowing blue brain** (Sørensen, 2023), robot hands à la "Creation of Adam", "code" backgrounds, Terminator: recommendations from **Better Images of AI** (Leverhulme CFI/Cambridge) and the **Oxford Internet Institute**. For a Brazilian project of sovereignty and inclusion, these clichés are contraindicated.
- **Use** (authentic HPC iconography): real **nodes, meshes, grids and network topologies**: *fat-tree* (the most used), torus (interconnected rings), hypercube, mesh. **SDumont** itself is the Tier-0 node of SINAPAD, with nodes linked by InfiniBand; after the 2024 upgrade (Eviden BullSequana XH3000, PBIA 2024–2028) it reached **18.85 petaflops**.

### 4. Brazilian and local symbolism

- **Alberto Santos-Dumont (1873–1932)**, "father of aviation", gives his name to the supercomputer. The public flight of the **14-Bis** (canard biplane) at Bagatelle/Paris, on **23/10/1906**, and the **220 m on 12/11/1906** are ratified milestones. The stylized wing honors this pioneering spirit **without** depicting a literal aircraft.
- **Geography**: the LNCC is in Petrópolis (RJ highlands); the technical education hub is in the Sul Fluminense region, near the Serra da Mantiqueira and the **Pico das Agulhas Negras (2,791 m)**. The relief (mountains, peak) dialogues with "reaching the heights": consistent with scientific aspiration.
- **Decolonial design** (Escobar, 2018, _Designs for the Pluriverse_): a design **situated in place**, which anchors identity in Brazilian imagery rather than importing technological imaginaries from the Global North.

### 5. Typography

- **Henderson, Giese & Cote (2004, _Journal of Marketing_)** and **Doyle & Bottomley (2004, _JBR_)**: the typeface was a statistically significant factor in brand choice; what matters is the **font × product** pairing. → a **geometric sans-serif with humanist details** (e.g., Inter, IBM Plex Sans, Archivo) communicates **modernity + reliability**, suitable for science/technology. "PEIA-HPC" in semibold weight; descriptor in regular.

## Technical specification

### Palette (Okabe-Ito · *color-blind-safe* · WCAG-aware)

| Role | Color | Hex | Application |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Blue | `#0072B2` | Wing structure; hub node (competence/trust) |
| Secondary | Teal | `#009E73` | Nodes/network (growth; distinction from generic tech-blue) |
| Accent | Amber | `#E69F00` | The **asymmetric** highlight **node** (energy/innovation) |
| Neutrals | Black / Gray | `#111111` / `#555555` | Wordmark and descriptor |

All primary/secondary/accent pairs maintain distinction under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia and in grayscale (distinct luminances).

### Construction

- Master grid **12×12**; wing on the **main diagonal**; node cluster in the **upper-right quadrant**.
- **Clear space** = the height of one node on all sides.
- **Minimum stroke width ≥ 1/48 of the total width** (ensures laser engraving and favicon).

### Versions

- **Main**: symbol + horizontal wordmark, polychromatic (`peia-hpc-logo.svg`).
- **Symbol**: icon only (`peia-hpc-simbolo.svg`).
- **Reduced/16 px favicon**: wing + 1 amber node (derive from the symbol).
- **Monochromatic**: single line, no gradients (`peia-hpc-simbolo-mono.svg`): laser, stamp, 1 color.

## Risks and caveats (what was weighed)

- **Evidence × transfer**: the effects of complexity, curvature, symmetry, naturalness, color and typography come from peer-reviewed experimental studies, **mostly on commercial brands**. The transfer to a scientific-educational identity is a reasonable inference, **not directly tested**. The "what to avoid in AI images" guidelines are academic critique, not causal experiments.
- **Misreading**: the wing may be read as "airline" if the nodes are not salient: this is why the graph receives **visual weight** and the amber node, prominence.
- **Color is contextual**: blue→trust is moderated by convention and culture; teal and amber exist to provide **distinctiveness**.
- **Trademark/prior-art**: before official use, an **INPI search** and validation of coexistence with **LNCC/SINAPAD/SDumont** is recommended (PEIA-HPC is a **user** project of the infrastructure, not the infrastructure; a joint attribution "carried out with support from SDumont/LNCC/SINAPAD" is suggested).

## Suggested next steps

1. **Robustness**: reduce the symbol to 16 px and to 1 color; confirm that it does not collapse.
2. **Accessibility**: simulate protanopia/deuteranopia/tritanopia and grayscale; verify contrast ≥ 3:1 (WCAG 1.4.11).
3. **Semiotic reading** (small n): show it to 8–12 people (technical students + researchers) and ask "what does this communicate?". If "aviation" dominates over "science/computing", increase the salience of the graph.
4. **Legal verification**: prior-art search at INPI; check against LNCC trademarks.
